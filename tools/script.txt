Why do the two pilots on your plane never eat the same meal? It sounds like a picky habit. It's actually one of the strangest safety rules in the sky. In nineteen seventy-five, a Japan Air Lines jumbo jet stopped in Anchorage, Alaska, to pick up breakfast. Ham omelets. But a cook had an infected cut on his hand, and the food sat too warm for days. Bacteria grew, and made a poison that reheating couldn't kill. Ninety minutes before Copenhagen, breakfast was served. And the plane turned into a nightmare. One hundred ninety-six passengers got violently sick. Over a hundred and forty ended up in the hospital. So who was flying the plane? The pilots were fine. Their body clocks were still on Alaska time, so they skipped breakfast. Not a single bite. That lucky skip saved everyone on board. So airlines made it a rule. If one pilot eats the chicken, the other eats the fish. Because if bad food knocks out one pilot, there's still someone left to land the plane. And that's why the two pilots on your plane never eat the same meal.
